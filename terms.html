4.1 How AI processing works

  • What is sent. To produce an answer, the app sends what that request needs through our servers to the AI model provider: your message, the project files relevant to it, pictures or files you attach or paste, and, for the studios, your descriptions and the structure of the model you're working on. When Auto-fix is on, compile errors, build logs and Minecraft crash reports are sent automatically so the Assistant can repair the mod. You can turn Auto-fix off in the app's settings. The Version Converter sends the source code and data files of the mod you choose to convert.
  • Reference material. To keep code accurate for your Minecraft version and mod loader, the app may add matching pages from its built-in Minecraft modding reference library to a request. They are part of the request's input like any other context.
  • Automatic actions cost Coins. Automatic repairs, verification steps and "Always continue" parts are AI requests too, and they cost Coins. The app shows them as they happen and stops after the number of attempts set in Settings. In the Texture Studio, every texture you generate - including each one in a batch, where up to five are painted at the same time - is its own AI request; higher Detail levels (Max, Ultra Detail) cost considerably more Coins and time.
  • No training on your work. We don't use your prompts, projects or AI output to train AI models, and we don't keep the content of requests or answers after they're delivered (see the Privacy Policy). AI providers process requests under their API terms and receive a random identifier for your account, never your name or email address.
  • No human review. AI answers are generated automatically and aren't checked by a person before you see them. Treat them as a draft: review, build and test before you use or share anything.
  • What you send is your responsibility. Don't put passwords, keys, other people's personal data or files you aren't allowed to share into requests or attachments.
